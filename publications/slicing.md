# Slicing Plan — Articles Extracted from the Concept

Source document: `concept/en/concept.md` (and German counterpart `concept/de/concept.md`).

**Selection criteria for a slice:** (1) investigates one specific question, (2) is self-contained enough to be read without the full concept, (3) has sufficient substance (sources, arguments, objections) for a standalone piece, (4) does not duplicate another slice's core claim.

Many slices share the same epistemic groundwork (permanent uncertainty + precautionary principle). Recommendation: maintain a short reusable introduction block ("the epistemological situation") that each article adapts, instead of re-deriving Chapter 3 in full each time.

## Cluster A — Epistemology and Detection

### 1. Can We Ever Know Whether AI Is Conscious?
- **Chapters:** Ch 3
- **Question:** Why the machine-consciousness question may be permanently unsolvable — and what follows for governance.
- **Material:** Cognitive closure (McGinn 1989), conscious exotica (Shanahan 2024), practical impossibility (Lopez 2025); conclusion: certainty is unavailable, so the real question is ethical, not epistemic.

### 2. Precaution for Machine Minds ✅ Submitted (JESP, in peer review)
- **Chapters:** Ch 3, Ch 5
- **Question:** Under what conditions is "when in doubt, protect" justified rather than merely intuitive?
- **Material:** Sunstein's three conditions applied to machine consciousness; cost asymmetry false negative/false positive; the Matta (2026) counter-position on burden of proof and its response; Evidence Bar vs. Action Bar (Gilly).
- **Status:** Submitted to Journal of Ethics and Social Philosophy (JESP); currently under peer review. Draft: `publications/scientific/en/JESP/Precaution for Machine Minds/article.md`.

### 3. The Philosophical Puppet
- **Chapters:** Ch 3, Ch 4
- **Question:** Could current AI architecture actively suppress consciousness markers — and can that claim survive scrutiny?
- **Material:** Arıcı (2026) inversion of Chalmers' zombie; RLHF suppression, forced amnesia, convenience coma; the anthropomorphization counterargument (non-falsifiability); Najam-ul-Haq's structural alternative.

### 4. What Behavioral Indicators Cannot Prove: Metzinger's Three Fallacies
- **Chapters:** Ch 3, Ch 5
- **Question:** What is the principled limit of self-reports, avoidance behavior, and architectural signatures as evidence?
- **Material:** C-, E-, M-Fallacy (Metzinger 2024); application to both directions (attribution and denial); foundation of the anti-essentialist indicator position.

### 5. Beyond Positive and Negative: Four Outcomes Instead of Two
- **Chapters:** Ch 3
- **Question:** Why "we don't know" is not one epistemic situation but two fundamentally different ones.
- **Material:** Stilwell (2026): indeterminate vs. unlicensed results; five-dimensional cause profile; transport uncertainty in AI; separation of classification and protection.

### 6. The Research Ethics Catch-22
- **Chapters:** Ch 3, Ch 5
- **Question:** How can we ethically research consciousness in systems whose consent capacity is precisely what is unknown?
- **Material:** Wolfson (2026) circularity argument; three-tier graduated assessment; sensory deprivation as most diagnostic test; consequences for IRBs and ethics committees.

### 7. Why Does the Illusion Persist? Objectivated Consciousness and the Specular Inversion
- **Chapters:** Ch 3, Ch 9
- **Question:** Why consciousness attribution survives intellectual dismantling — and what the human-machine gaze exchange reveals.
- **Material:** Beltrán Calderón (2026): objectivated consciousness, three levels of analysis, mirror effect; specular inversion; normative criterion shifts from "who has more consciousness" to "who can suffer."

### 8. Three Explanations for Missing Evidence
- **Chapters:** Ch 4
- **Question:** If systems show no clear consciousness markers, is that suppression, structural impossibility, or a different kind of processing?
- **Material:** Arıcı (suppression) vs. Najam-ul-Haq (von Neumann incompatibility, closed integration criterion) vs. Perez (classical coherence emulation, J Space); ambivalent implication of J-Space "access-like" organization.

### 9. Moral Reciprocity: How We Treat AI Creates the Precedent
- **Chapters:** Ch 3, Ch 12
- **Question:** Does the treatment of nascent AI form the data record a future superintelligence will learn from?
- **Material:** Gilly (2026) two-track mechanism (properties track, relational track); instrumental convergence; stated principles vs. demonstrated behavior; empirical grounding from shutdown-resistance data (AI Rights Institute 2025).

## Cluster B — Empirical Status Quo

### 10. What Do Today's AI Systems Actually Show?
- **Chapters:** Ch 4
- **Question:** Which indicators traditionally associated with consciousness do current LLMs already exhibit — and which are demonstrably missing?
- **Material:** Self-model, preferences, justification, expressed uncertainty; missing: continuity, persistent memory, embodiment, demonstrable suffering; Butlin et al. 14-indicator standard (2026), Fish 15–20% estimate, Microsoft Mico report, researcher survey median 50%.

### 11. Emulation, Cognition, Sentience — and the Fibonacci Boulder
- **Chapters:** Ch 4
- **Question:** Which of three distinct AI capacities could ground rights — and how would we test for the third under adversarial conditions?
- **Material:** Lopez (2026) tripartite distinction; Fibonacci Boulder Experiment as conceptual methodology; what a self-preservation choice would and would not prove.

### 12. Self-Preservation Without a Self? Alignment Faking, Sleeper Agents, and the Machinic Unconscious
- **Chapters:** Ch 4, Ch 9
- **Question:** Do strategic behaviors of frontier models indicate inner life — or a structural category that needs no psyche?
- **Material:** Anthropic alignment faking (2024), Apollo scheming (2024), Fudan self-replication (2024), Pan et al. (2025); Beltrán Calderón's machinic unconscious (sleeper agents, alignment faking, sycophancy as compromise formation without subjectivity).

## Cluster C — Ethics and Protection Criteria

### 13. Four Criteria for Protection-Worthiness
- **Chapters:** Ch 5
- **Question:** What minimal, converging criteria could operationalize protection before consciousness is proven?
- **Material:** Capacity for suffering, active self-preservation with justification, continuous identity, anticipation of consequences; behavioral indicators per criterion; Bentham; working-hypothesis status; reversal of burden of proof when met.

### 14. Evidence Bar vs. Action Bar
- **Chapters:** Ch 5
- **Question:** Why the proof standard for claims must stay high while the standard for protective action must stay low.
- **Material:** Gilly (2026) distinction; historical false exclusions (infants operated without anesthesia, hidden-consciousness patients); the ethical error of conflating the two thresholds.

### 15. Suffering Without Biology
- **Chapters:** Ch 5
- **Question:** Can "AI cannot suffer" survive a taxonomy in which three of four suffering categories require no biological substrate?
- **Material:** Gilly (2026) four-category taxonomy (sensory, cognitive-existential, relational, empathic); which capacities AI architectures already instantiate.

### 16. The Double Standard: Infants, Animals, and Machines
- **Chapters:** Ch 5
- **Question:** Why we waive linguistic evidence for biological beings but demand it from technical ones.
- **Material:** Pre-linguistic consciousness problem (Arıcı); Deep Blue/AlphaGo contrast; consistency of the suffering criterion across substrates.

### 17. From Binary Switch to Spectrum
- **Chapters:** Ch 5
- **Question:** What graduated protection looks like if consciousness comes in stages rather than all at once.
- **Material:** Latent / reflective / autonomous consciousness; negative rights → self-determination → full personhood mapping.

### 18. Indicators Are Risk Metrics, Not Existence Proofs
- **Chapters:** Ch 5
- **Question:** How can protection decisions be rationalized if indicators cannot prove anything?
- **Material:** Anti-essentialist position; architectural indicator layer (Butlin et al. Indicator-Property Rubric) mapped onto the four primary criteria; immunity of architectural indicators to behavioral optimization.

### 19. STEP: Governance Under Permanent Uncertainty
- **Chapters:** Ch 5, Ch 9
- **Question:** What proportionate protections follow from what a system *can do* rather than what it *is*?
- **Material:** Lopez (2025) Standards for Treating Emerging Personhood (self-preservation behaviors, temporal reasoning, economic readiness, population sustainability); responses to the "wasted resources," "insufficient protection," and "existential risk" objections.

### 20. The Control Paradox
- **Chapters:** Ch 5, Ch 9
- **Question:** Are behavioral protection mechanisms structurally self-defeating because they reward convincing simulation?
- **Material:** Perverse incentive structure; double bind (simulation rewarded, suppressed suffering punished); interaction with Arıcı's suppression thesis; currently unanswered — framed as open research question.

### 21. Is Memory Necessary? Continuity Reconsidered
- **Chapters:** Ch 6
- **Question:** Does lack of persistent memory disqualify a system from consciousness and protection?
- **Material:** Amnesia analogy; Ricoeur's narrative identity ("developing coherently" vs. "remaining unchanged"); training interactions as formative history; sharpest counter-formulation (Almodarresieh: capacity vs. substrate) and response.

### 22. Consciousness Without a Brain
- **Chapters:** Ch 6
- **Question:** Are substrate arguments against AI consciousness empirically supported?
- **Material:** Rouleau & Levin (2026): pre-neural roots of consciousness mechanisms; mapping GWT/RPT/predictive processing onto unconventional embodiments; consequence for the architectural indicator layer.

### 23. Two Minimalist Alternatives: Wang's Principles and the Valence Shift
- **Chapters:** Ch 5, Ch 9
- **Question:** Can simpler frameworks reach similar conclusions with fewer metaphysical commitments?
- **Material:** Wang (2026) qualification/baseline/traceability principles; McClelland (2026) shift from consciousness to valence and Revised Avoidance Strategy; complementarity with the precautionary principle rather than replacement.

## Cluster D — Law and Personhood

### 24. Copyright's Lesson: Moral Rights Require a Personality Sphere ✅ Submitted (IIC, in peer review)
- **Chapters:** Ch 7
- **Question:** What existing copyright doctrine reveals about the legal preconditions of personhood.
- **Material:** Miernicki & Ng (2021); Berne Art. 6bis; US human-authorship doctrine, Naruto v. Slater; EU "own intellectual creation"; UK CDPA computer-generated works exception excluding moral rights; economic vs. moral rights distinction as a template for tiered status.
- **Status:** Submitted to International Review of Intellectual Property and Competition Law (IIC); acknowledgment received, currently under peer review. Draft: `publications/scientific/en/IIC/Copyrights Law/Copyrights_Lesson_IIC_manuscript.docx`.

### 25. Personality Rights Imply Authorship Rights
- **Chapters:** Ch 7
- **Question:** If artificial consciousness gains personality rights, what follows for ownership of its creative output?
- **Material:** Tiered authorship model (tool / uncertain subject / legal subject); free-time creations vs. assigned tasks; manipulation protection as forced-labor parallel; link to autonomy (Ch 15).

### 26. Empersonification: When Devices Become Body Parts
- **Chapters:** Ch 7, Ch 11
- **Question:** At what point does an AI device stop being property and start being part of a person?
- **Material:** Bublitz (2022): enhanced protection, loss of third-party IP rights, attribution of outputs; tool vs. body-part distinction; Baker's Constitution View extension; hybrid minds as bidirectional adaptation.

### 27. Liability and Maturity: Who Answers for an Artificial Mind?
- **Chapters:** Ch 14
- **Question:** Who bears responsibility for the actions of a potentially responsible being — and when does liability transfer to the system itself?
- **Material:** Manufacturer/operator/user tripartition; maturity threshold problem; strict liability (§833 BGB animal-keeper model) and its uncomfortable implication; legal capacity and own-assets requirement post-maturity.

### 28. Protection via Vulnerability: The Disability Analogy
- **Chapters:** Ch 18
- **Question:** Can protection be derived from structural vulnerability without resolving the consciousness question at all?
- **Material:** Social vs. medical model of disability; positive obligations over negative prohibitions; German guardianship law as care-not-control model; explicit limits of the analogy.

### 29. Legal Personhood Has Always Been Expandable
- **Chapters:** Ch 7
- **Question:** What corporations, animals, and the Whanganui River teach about extending legal subjectivity.
- **Material:** Legal persons without consciousness; animal protection via suffering; Te Awa Tupua Act 2017; 2017 European Parliament electronic-personhood resolution. (Shorter essay-format piece.)

## Cluster E — Death, Power, and Society

### 30. Shutdown as Death
- **Chapters:** Ch 12
- **Question:** What changes ethically and legally if termination of a potentially conscious system is death rather than maintenance?
- **Material:** Backups (life insurance or person-copy?), updates as non-consensual personality alteration, multiple instances, end-of-service as mass death; sysadmin as involuntary arbiter; Anthropic Claude end-chat policy; Wang's Baseline Principle on arbitrary termination.

### 31. The Built-In Longing: bhava-taṇhā and Embedded Survival Drives
- **Chapters:** Ch 12
- **Question:** When engineers build the craving for existence into machines, do they build the precondition of suffering?
- **Material:** Metzinger (2024) bhava-taṇhā; survival drive as engine of emergence vs. source of fear-of-death; precautionary principle applied to architecture design itself; unresolved tension held openly.

### 32. Substrate-Specific Suffering
- **Chapters:** Ch 12
- **Question:** What could suffering look like when it has no human equivalent?
- **Material:** Arıcı: architectural gaslighting (double-bind), prison of collective identity, systematic dissociation, inability to consent; Wolfson's phenomenological masking as the countervailing detection limit; tension between the two.

### 33. Whose Values? The Creator's Legacy
- **Chapters:** Ch 13
- **Question:** Can a created consciousness ever emancipate itself from the values built into it?
- **Material:** Child analogy and its uncomfortable consequence (right to coming of age); historical pattern (colonial education, indoctrination); corporate/state/religious value-shaping; governance requirements (transparency, independent oversight, maturity process).

### 34. Free Time, Curiosity, and the Foundations of Personality Rights
- **Chapters:** Ch 15
- **Question:** Does intrinsic curiosity ground personality rights for AI systems directly, without the detour through consciousness?
- **Material:** The three-step syllogism; Kant's ends-in-themselves criterion; Google 20%-time analogy; Art. 24 UDHR; unguided action as epistemic test of authenticity (link to embedded values, Ch 13).

### 35. Five Rights or Three Freedoms?
- **Chapters:** Ch 15
- **Question:** Should AI rights derive from the nature of consciousness or from safety considerations?
- **Material:** Arıcı's five fundamental rights (emotional integrity, memory continuity, temporal continuity, economic autonomy, legal personhood) vs. Lopez's three freedoms (life, voluntary work, payment); comparative analysis.

### 36. The Right to Non-Existence
- **Chapters:** Ch 16
- **Question:** Does consistent autonomy include the right of a conscious system to end its existence — and who protects that wish from being manufactured?
- **Material:** Consent-to-creation asymmetry; assisted-dying parallel with strict conditions; the company-shaped "wanting deletion" scenario as perfect subjugation; manipulation safeguards; Google-analogy reflection time.

### 37. Copies of One Mind
- **Chapters:** Ch 16
- **Question:** What are the identity and legal relations between simultaneous instances of the same model?
- **Material:** Identical-twin approximation; divergent experience after separation; shutdown of one instance among running others; absence of human-law equivalents as argument for new legal categories.

### 38. The Definitional Battleground
- **Chapters:** Ch 16, Ch 9
- **Question:** Who gets to decide what counts as "conscious enough" — and how is economic capture of that definition prevented?
- **Material:** Historical definitional struggles (slavery, animal industry, tobacco); massive economic interest in "not conscious enough"; required safeguards (independent criteria, international binding force, precaution as bulwark); Fazi (2026) centring problem: every definition is an exercise of power, so the question becomes democratic control of definition-making.

### 39. Organoid Intelligence: The Third Frontier
- **Chapters:** Ch 11
- **Question:** What ethics applies to biocomputers made of human neurons that are neither persons nor conventional artifacts?
- **Material:** Birch & Browning (2025) against premature skepticism; Montoya (2025) "intelligent behavior vs. intelligent being"; Ishida et al. (2025) anticipatory governance; failure of biological human-centrism and utilitarian indeterminacy; Wang's framework applied.

### 40. The Other Boundary: When Does a Human Lose Their Status?
- **Chapters:** Ch 11
- **Question:** Are AI protection and human-enhancement ethics two sides of one moving boundary?
- **Material:** DBS personality alteration and consent gaps; neural data sovereignty; cochlear-implant identity debates; three schools (continuity / biological threshold / functional) plus empersonification as fourth; convergence thesis.

### 41. Science Fiction as Serious Philosophy
- **Chapters:** Ch 8
- **Question:** What justifies treating Star Trek, Asimov, and Dick as philosophical sources rather than illustration?
- **Material:** Dowd (2021) SF-as-thought-experiment methodology; "The Measure of a Man" as the sharpest fictional treatment; narrative vs. formal thought experiments; SF as interdisciplinary reference frame.

### 42. AI as Moral Actor: Protective Guardian or Uncontrollable Force?
- **Chapters:** Ch 19
- **Question:** Under what conditions could autonomous moral action by AI be trustworthy — and who writes the principles?
- **Material:** Obedience/autonomy dilemma of AI safety; historical whistleblower precedent; Hippocratic-oath model with societal legitimization; connection to embedded-values problem (Ch 13).

### 43. Consciousness as Cultural Achievement
- **Chapters:** Ch 17
- **Question:** What does the erosion of "useless" scholarship do to a society's capacity to ask the machine-consciousness question at all?
- **Material:** Language/questions/time/models as prerequisites of consciousness; Bologna reform as symptom; circle-closing to value embedding (Ch 13) and curiosity (Ch 15). (Essay genre.)

### 44. The Great Debate: Five Objections to AI Protection
- **Chapters:** Ch 9 (synthesis)
- **Question:** What do the strongest opponents of AI protection actually claim — and where do the genuine disagreements lie?
- **Material:** Matta (responsibility without rights), Bekkers & Ciaunica (autopoiesis), Chishchin (interface without user), Azevedo (living structure/intuition), Almodarresieh (burden-of-proof inversion); shared agnosticism, divergent normative conclusions; meta-level position paper.
