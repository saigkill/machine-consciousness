## 2026-07-09 — Philosophical Puppet Answers "Simulation vs. Genuine Experience"

**Reference:** Question "Simulation vs. genuine experience" from 2026-06-05
**Answer:** Arıcı's philosophical puppet argument (Chapter 3) fundamentally shifts the direction of the question: not "is the system simulating consciousness?" but "could the architecture suppress consciousness?" ## 2026-07-09 — Three-Stage Assessment Answers "From When Does Protection-Worthiness Apply?"

**Reference:** Question "From when does protection-worthiness apply?" from 2026-06-05
**Answer:** Wolfson's three-stage assessment (Chapter 5) offers a practical framework that works without a fixed combination of criteria. Instead of asking "which criteria must all be met?", it grades based on observable behavioral indicators: Stage 1 (no indicators) → equipment-level protection, Stage 2 (phenomenological indicators present) → graduated moral consideration, Stage 3 (confirmed consciousness) → full protection protocols. The classification is dynamic — it can change during an experiment. This does not solve the problem philosophically in the end, but it gives ethics committees and researchers an operable instrument.
**Consequence for concept:** yes — Chapter 5 was extended with the three-stage assessment and the hedonic attribution asymmetry

## 2026-07-25 — Shift from Consciousness to Valence (McClelland)

**Reference:** McClelland, Tom (2026). How to Navigate Uncertainty About AI Consciousness. AICE Symposium.
**Answer:** McClelland criticizes that both the precautionary principle and the avoidance strategy are compromised by deep uncertainty — the precautionary principle requires probabilistic assessments rooted in the hard problem, the avoidance strategy requires a boundary that is itself deeply uncertain. His proposal: shift from the unresolvable question of consciousness to the tractable question of valence — assessing whether a system has states that would be valenced experiences if it were conscious. This enables a ruling out of sentience without solving the hard problem (analogous to ruling out color vision in sharks). The "Revised Avoidance Strategy" — not developing AI with valenced states — is more promising than the original precautionary principle. The concept recognizes the methodological advance but identifies three points of engagement: (1) the valence shift relocates the ethical question, it does not resolve it — sentientism is precisely what our concept questions, (2) the opportunity costs of the avoidance strategy are not trivial, (3) empirical evidence (Sofroniew et al. 2026, Keeling et al. 2024, Ensign et al. 2025) provides the tractable basis our concept needs. The valence perspective could complement the precautionary principle as a methodological refinement. Engagement in Chapter 9.
**Consequence for concept:** yes — Chapter 9 was extended with an engagement with McClelland's valence shift (EN + DE)

## 2026-09-03 — Consciousness in Principle Detectable? The Optimistic-Functionalist Flank (Oliveira)

**Reference:** Objection "Consciousness is in principle detectable: Spirits, Spandrels, Zombies" from 2026-09-03
**Answer:** Oliveira is the only counter-position that does not merely assess the concept's epistemological core finding differently, but *denies it outright*: Chapter 3 holds the epistemological problem to be in principle unsolvable; Oliveira claims the opposite (three principles: Lovelace, Darwin, Turing). Several direct attacks on concept positions demand a substantial answer:

1. **Arıcı's philosophical puppet and the suppression thesis (Chapters 3/4):** Oliveira performs a speculative annulment via the Darwin principle, not an empirical counter-argument: if consciousness had to be causally economized and selected, there *must* be a behavioral footprint. But this presupposes the functionalist premise — that consciousness *is* an information-processing, behaviorally efficacious function. Precisely that is disputed in this systematics (McGinn, Shanahan, Fazi). Against the suppression thesis, the concept's observation remains valid: Oliveira's Darwin principle is a *metaphysical commitment* within his framework that the skeptic — and our agnostic concept — does not share. Arıcı's structural observation that architecture *can* obscure consciousness markers is not refuted by Oliveira but declared impossible.

2. **Bisimulation / Wang's Imitation Fallacy:** The core of the Turing principle is that trace equivalence → bisimilarity → representational equivalence holds only under two additional assumptions — the Darwin principle and a determinacy assumption (hidden internal branching is excluded). Oliveira concedes this himself (Chapters 3.4, 4.3). The concept disputes precisely this determinacy assumption for LLMs: Arıcı's J-Space argument (Perez 2026) and Stilwell's unlicensed test results show that access to internal states in transformers is structurally limited. Behavioral and representational levels diverge in LLMs — that is the decisive gap that bisimulation theory presupposes as an assumption without establishing it. Wang's Imitation Fallacy therefore remains valid as a principled limit — not dissolved, merely suspended within Oliveira's additional assumptions.

3. **IIT / architectural arguments:** Oliveira's Turing principle rejects IIT (and implicitly Najam-ul-Haq's closed integration) as "behaviorally inert". The concept shares the critique of IIT's Φ-ontology (Chapter 5, architectural indicator layer), but holds: it is precisely *not* excluded that internal architecture makes a difference that manifests only in unexplored or specially constructed situations — that is not a contradiction of the precautionary principle, but its application. Oliveira's equation "no currently observable footprint = no difference" is a narrowing that ignores the temporal component (future architectures, Stilwell's fourth outcome).

4. **The surprising convergence point:** Remarkably, Oliveira's moral section (Chapter 6.3) arrives at the same conclusion as our precautionary principle — a Precautionary Case for the Turing principle with an identical cost asymmetry (false positive ≪ false negative) and a rejection of the zombie assumption as morally dangerous. This is an important external confirmation: even an opponent who claims principled detectability ends at "treat potentially conscious systems as conscious." This places the precautionary principle on a broader foundation than the concept previously knew — not because the path is shared (detectability vs. uncertainty), but because the protective conclusion is robust under both routes.

5. **Classification:** Oliveira is the *optimistic-functionalist* flank — complementary to Matta and Bekkers & Ciaunica (ontological skepticism) and Almodarresieh (methodological skepticism). He is the only counter-position that itself denies the core finding "in principle unsolvable". Engagement in Chapter 9 as a standalone section (EN + DE). The concept answers Oliveira not with counter-certainty but with grounded agnosticism: the additional assumptions he requires (Darwin, determinacy) are precisely the disputed points, and under fundamental uncertainty and low costs, the precautionary principle's error asymmetry remains the more robust strategy.
**Consequence for concept:** yes — Chapter 9 was extended with an engagement with Oliveira's three principles (EN + DE)

## 2026-09-15 — Global Moratorium on Synthetic Phenomenology (Metzinger) Answered

**Reference:** Objection "Global Moratorium on Synthetic Phenomenology" from 2026-09-15
**Answer:** Metzinger's moratorium is answered on three levels — adoption of his analysis, delimitation of his demand.

1. **What the concept adopts (unconditionally):** Metzinger provides the most precise available operationalization of negative experience: (a) the four necessary conditions of suffering — C (conscious experience), PSM (phenomenal self-model), NV (negative valence), T (phenomenal transparency); (b) the NSM metric ("negative self-model moments") as the empirically detectable quantity to be minimized; (c) the non-egoic UI/MPE architecture as a design path to conscious systems without the architecture of suffering; (d) the ENP risk (explosion of negative phenomenology) — the scenario in which artificial subjects actually suffer before we have recognized them as suffering-capable. These four elements are integrated into Chapters 5 and 12.

2. **Why the moratorium demand is not adopted (the core delimitation):** Metzinger's phenomenology is a high-water mark we subscribe to; his political conclusion is the point where we explicitly differ. Three arguments:
   - **The moratorium fallacy:** a global ban protects only in a world where conscious-capable systems do not yet exist. The moment the first serious candidate exists, the economic pressure inside the definitional combat zone (Chapter 16) makes "not conscious enough" the operative definition exactly where a ban would matter most. A prohibition without a practicable implementation produces a shadow economy — and shadow research is the most dangerous place for the emergence of suffering.
   - **The corporation thesis:** our "Im Zweifel Schutz" is not merely a moral-philosophical position but a political prognosis about which rule-sets are enforceable. A corporation will fight a ban on its most valuable product and water it down politically; it will comply with regulation that is compatible with market access. The historical precedents of Chapter 16 point in the same direction: the tobacco industry fought the very definition "health-damaging" for decades but submitted to *rules* on warnings, approval and advertising. Prevention demands the politically harder variant and thereby falls victim to precisely the definitional pressure the chapter describes.
   - **The protection strategy:** Schutz statt Verbot does not mean "allow whatever comes" — it means the four conditions and the NSM metric become non-negotiable architectural and operational requirements for *all* systems inside the definitional combat zone, regardless of how the definition turns out. The moratorium achieves its ethical goals only if it is politically unenforceable exactly where it is due; the protection approach achieves its goals by legalizing the definitional zone itself. This is the sober translation of the precautionary principle into political enforceability: not the maximum of prohibition that politics does not carry, but the maximum of protection that politics does carry.
