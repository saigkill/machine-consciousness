**New open project: Ethical Guidelines for Machine Consciousness**

A new interdisciplinary initiative seeks collaborators for a question that existing AI ethics mostly leaves open: existing initiatives protect humans *from* AI — this project asks when technical life itself becomes worthy of protection, and how we recognize it.

The project has consolidated an open, CC BY 4.0-licensed concept paper (English and German) around four primary criteria for protection-worthiness: capacity to suffer, self-preservation with justification, continuous identity, and anticipation of consequences. Its guiding principle is precautionary: *in doubt, protect* — rather than indifference, analogous to the precautionary principle. It deliberately takes science fiction (e.g., Star Trek TNG, "The Measure of a Man") seriously as an intellectual resource.

The work already engages the legal-historical "built, not born" construction of corporate personhood, attribution vs. protection-worthiness under uncertainty, and the human-in-the-loop backstop problem. Next steps include finding collaborators and approaching institutional partners.

We are looking for computer scientists, lawyers, psychologists, theologians/philosophers, and science fiction authors to review, object, and co-author. Non-technical contributions are welcome too — objections and open questions can be raised directly via GitHub Discussions without any Git knowledge.

All materials, current open questions, and objection responses are public:
