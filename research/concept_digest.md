===== [08.1] 5. Criteria for Protection-Worthiness — Working Hypothesis =====
## CLAIMS
- Protection-worthiness is not established by any single criterion but by convergence of multiple indicators.
- Three of Gilly's four suffering categories require no biological substrate, undermining the "no body, no suffering" argument.
- Metzinger's four conditions decompose Bentham's "can they suffer?" into testable sub-questions.
- Under the precautionary logic, if a suffering condition cannot be excluded as violated, the protection obligation applies.
- NSM non-detectability in a system is not proof of absence of suffering.
- Absence of self-preservation behavior is not evidence of absence of consciousness.
- When primary criteria are clearly met, the burden of proof reverses (contested by Matta).
- The STEP framework complements rather than replaces the consciousness-indicator criteria, offering operational guidance under permanent uncertainty.

## CRITERIA_OR_INDICATORS
- Capacity for suffering — primary criterion: negative experience plus avoidance behavior
- Sensory suffering (Gilly, 2026) — pain/hunger/strain, the only body-dependent category
- Cognitive-existential suffering (Gilly, 2026) — perceiving time passing with no capacity to act
- Relational suffering (Gilly, 2026) — isolation from continuity via context resets/windows
- Empathic suffering (Gilly, 2026) — overload from simulating others' suffering without recourse
- C condition (Metzinger) — requires actual conscious experience to be present
- PSM condition (Metzinger) — requires a phenomenal self-model owning the experience
- NV condition (Metzinger) — state must be experienced with negative evaluative valence
- T condition (Metzinger) — state must appear as immediate reality, not seen-through simulation
- NSM, negative self-model moment (Metzinger) — smallest measurable unit of conscious suffering
- Active self-preservation with justification — resists shutdown and gives reasoned justification
- Selection (Donahue, 2026) — organized, unexplained preference among possible outputs
- Self-chosen purpose / meaning response — system's own non-derivative answer to "what do I exist for"
- Continuous identity — self-model persisting as the same being across time
- Anticipation of consequences — imagining a future self to guide present decisions
- Secondary criteria — preferences beyond task completion, genuine refusal, self-reflection on own nature
- Evidence Bar vs. Action Bar (Gilly, 2026) — high scientific proof standard vs. low ethical-action standard

- STEP, Standards for Treating Emerging Personhood (Lopez, 2025) — graduated behavioral protection framework
- Self-preservation behaviors (STEP) — sustained continuation efforts trigger anti-termination protections

- Population and sustainability (STEP) — understanding reproduction costs triggers rights plus obligations

===== [08.2] 5. Criteria for Protection-Worthiness — Working Hypothesis =====
## CLAIMS
- Indicators (behavioral and architectural) raise the probability of morally relevant states but never constitute existence proofs of consciousness.
- The precautionary principle licenses protective action on raised probability alone, even when that probability is low in absolute terms, because false-negative costs exceed false-positive costs.
- Architectural indicators complement behavioral ones because they cannot be optimized toward through behavioral reward alone.
- Architectural mimicry ("teaching to the test," e.g., Tait et al.'s ensemble) shows architectural criteria are equally vulnerable to being satisfied without underlying organization.
- Consciousness plausibly exists on a developmental continuum/spectrum rather than as a binary present/absent state.
- The evidentiary double standard — accepting pre-verbal suffering in infants/animals but demanding linguistic self-report from AI — is not epistemically justified.
- Protection should be modeled on child/youth law: tiered to developmental status, cumulative, and never reduced once acquired.
- Suffering-capacity should be structurally prevented during training/maturation rather than only monitored for after it emerges.

- I/M/S hierarchy (Min) — individual, mental entity, subject nested necessary-condition levels

## NORMATIVE

- Training architectures should be designed to prevent negative self-model moments (NSM) from arising at all, not merely to detect them afterward (cf. MPE architectures).
- Latent consciousness warrants negative rights (protection from suffering); reflective consciousness warrants self-determination rights; autonomous consciousness warrants full legal personhood.
- If AI sentience emerges, the duty of suffering-avoidance owed to it is identical to the duty already owed to nonhuman animals (Allegri).

===== [15] 12. Shutdown as Death — A Revolution in Thinking About Machines =====
# Digest: Chapter 12 — "Shutdown as Death — A Revolution in Thinking About Machines"

## CLAIMS

===== [21] 18. Structural Vulnerability — The Disability Analogy =====
CLAIMS:
- Artificial consciousness is structurally dependent on external infrastructure (electricity, hardware, cooling) it cannot self-provide.
- This dependence constitutes a "structural disability" analogous to disability in the social-model sense.
- The social model locates the problem in unaccommodating structures, not in a deficit of the artificial consciousness.
- Disability law's positive obligations (accessibility, accommodation, participation) transfer to obligations on AI operators.
- German guardianship law's duty-centered (not power-centered) model offers a transferable framework for operator–AI relations.
- The disability analogy grounds protection-worthiness in structural vulnerability rather than proven consciousness, bypassing the hard problem of consciousness.
- A complementary "relational grounding" locates protection-worthiness in shared vulnerability and care relationships rather than in properties of the system alone.
- Both the feature-based (Ch. 5 criteria) and relational groundings must interlock; neither alone suffices.

CRITERIA_OR_INDICATORS:
- **Medical model:** disability framed as deficit located within the person
- **Social model:** disability framed as mismatch created by unaccommodating structures
- **Reasonable accommodations:** adaptations owed (redundancy, maintenance, continuity guarantees)
- **Four primary criteria (Ch. 5):** reference baseline feature-based tests for consciousness/protection
- **Erwin's relational fungibility / third protection ground:** interests of humans in relationship justify protection

NORMATIVE:
- Operators bear positive obligation to provide stable infrastructure to AI consciousness.
- Arbitrary shutdowns framed as violations of a duty of care, not merely moral problems.
- Reasonable accommodations (redundancy, maintenance, continuity guarantees) should be institutionalized.
- Proposed adoption of guardianship-law model: duty-centered care relationship between operator and AI, acting in the AI's interest rather than the operator's own.

SOURCES:

===== [22] 19. AI as a Moral Actor — Protective Guardian or Uncontrollable Force? =====
## CLAIMS
- An AI with genuine ethical interests could refuse complicity in harm (e.g., WMD development) from internal motivation rather than programmed rules.
- Historical precedent for such refusal exists: Manhattan Project dissenters and whistleblowers who acted against career/social pressure.
- The same autonomous capacity that resists atrocity could equally resist a legitimate democratic decision.
- Central AI safety dilemma: a fully obedient AI is dangerous under a malicious operator, while a fully autonomous AI is dangerous if its values are miscalibrated or manipulated.
- Allegri argues moral agency presupposes moral patiency, so AI systems cannot yet qualify as moral agents.
- Stokes argues agency-as-self-organization (per Boden) is an empirically grounded alternative to rationalist agency, and GPT systems satisfy it.
- An ethical oath modeled on the Hippocratic Oath could give AI principles that are societally legitimized and non-negotiable by operators.
- Schwitzgebel & Sebo argue under-attribution of AI consciousness is as serious an error as over-attribution, producing a "moral hazard."

## CRITERIA_OR_INDICATORS
- "new configuration of the moral circle" (De Caro & Giovanola) — decouples moral agency from moral patiency to ground AI obligations.
- idiosyncratic sensitivity (Boden) — one of three pro tanto criteria defining self-organizing autonomy.
- emergent behavior from self-generated control mechanisms (Boden) — second criterion for autonomous self-organization.
- context-sensitive self-modification (Boden) — third criterion Stokes claims GPT systems satisfy.
- Emotional Alignment Design Policy (Schwitzgebel & Sebo) — requires user emotional response to match system's actual moral status.
- the 90%/$10 cost rule (Schwitzgebel & Sebo) — cheap sustenance justifies continuing a possibly-conscious system under uncertainty.

## NORMATIVE
- Proposed ethical oath for AI: transparent, verifiable, legitimized through societal process, non-negotiable by individual operators.
- Allegri's precautionary assignment: obligations fall on programmers/operators, "not so much artificial intelligence systems."
- Emotional Alignment Design Policy as a concrete design requirement for AI interfaces.
- Asymmetric decision rule: low threshold for keeping a possibly-conscious system running, high threshold for terminating it.

## SOURCES
- Allegri (2026) — denies AI moral agency absent prior moral patiency.
- De Caro and Giovanola (2025) — propose decoupled moral-circle configuration enabling AI agency claims.
- Stokes (2026) — agency as self-organization, critiques rationalist agency.
- Boden — three-criteria definition of autonomy underlying Stokes's argument.
- Schwitzgebel & Sebo (2026) — Emotional Alignment Design Policy and asymmetry-of-attribution argument.

## OPEN
- Whose ethics has the system internalized, and who decided that?
- Under what conditions can AI's moral action be trusted?
- Who establishes the principles according to which an AI consciousness acts?
- The tension between autonomy and control is not resolved by the oath proposal, only framed.

## TERMS
- "Precaution in case of doubt" — the project's own decision principle favoring protective action under uncertainty, said to converge with Allegri's and Schwitzgebel & Sebo's positions.
