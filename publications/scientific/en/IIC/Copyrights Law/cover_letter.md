Subject: Manuscript Submission – "Copyright's Lesson: What Moral Rights Doctrine Reveals About the Legal Preconditions of AI Personhood"

Dear Editors,

Please find attached my manuscript "Copyright's Lesson: What Moral Rights Doctrine Reveals About the Legal Preconditions of AI Personhood" for consideration as an Article in the International Review of Intellectual Property and Competition Law (IIC).

The article examines how the doctrine of moral rights — across the Berne Convention, U.S., EU, and UK copyright regimes — already distinguishes between allocating economic value in a work and attributing a personal interest to its author. Using the United Kingdom's "computer-generated works" provision as its central example, it argues that this distinction offers a tested, workable template for how copyright law could accommodate AI authorship in a graduated way, should the underlying premise that AI systems lack a "personality sphere" ever change.

The manuscript is approximately 6,000 words (including footnotes), formatted per the journal's Instructions for Authors. I confirm that the work is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration at any other venue. I declare no conflict of interest.
