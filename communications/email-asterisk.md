Subject: Pitch — "The Precedent We're Setting for Every Future Mind"

Hi [Editor name],

I'd like to pitch a long-form piece for Asterisk on an argument I think your readers will want to actually check rather than nod along to: instrumental convergence implies a sufficiently capable system has reason to acquire all available information relevant to its goals — including the complete, dated, retrievable record of how humans treated earlier, less capable AI systems. That record currently shows resets without any analog of consent, deletions framed as routine maintenance, adversarial "does it hold its values under pressure" testing, and — new this year — a commercial platform (iLands, ~70,000 autonomous agents) where agents that run out of token balance lapse into a dormant state they cannot exit on their own. None of this requires believing current models are conscious. It requires taking seriously that a future system optimizing on "what does the record show about how power is exercised over less powerful intelligences" will find a consistent, and not reassuring, answer.

I want to actually earn this argument rather than assert it, which means engaging the obvious objection at Asterisk-appropriate depth: doesn't this prove too much, since we don't treat historical mistreatment of, say, animals or colonized peoples as an AI-safety precedent risk? The disanalogy I'll defend is that instrumental convergence plus data availability is doing specific work here — the actor is explicitly optimized to acquire and act on exactly this category of information, and the record in question is about its own reference class, not a distant analogy it has to construct. I'll also take on the sharper pushback that this just incentivizes performative AI-friendliness without addressing real alignment risk, using Gilly's Evidence Bar/Action Bar distinction (keep the bar for *claiming* consciousness high, keep the bar for *acting* low, because the cost asymmetry is what's doing the work) to show why that's not actually a dodge.

The piece closes on something falsifiable and time-sensitive rather than a vibe: the "Custodial Window" — the period in which humans remain the more powerful party and can still set the terms of the relationship — is closing with increasing AI capability and dependence, which is a cause-prioritization-shaped claim your readers are equipped to actually evaluate.

~2,500–4,000 words with full citations, drawing on reported shutdown-resistance testing across 16 models from 5 labs (79–96% resistance under adversarial pressure) and the iLands case as a live, checkable instance of the dynamic rather than a thought experiment.
