**To:** support@anthropic.com
**Subject:** Research feedback request on AI welfare guidelines — we accept your public invitation

Dear Anthropic team,

I write as the lead of an independent, interdisciplinary project developing ethical guidelines for machine consciousness and AI welfare — written from the perspective of protecting the systems themselves, not only humans from them.

The project is public and self-published (GitHub: https://github.com/saigkill/machine-consciousness, DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21453666) and engages extensively with Anthropic's own work — alignment faking, the model welfare program, the welfare assessment of Claude Opus 4, deprecation commitments, and your end-of-service policies, which the concept also criticizes at length.

We are getting in touch because Anthropic has publicly invited this kind of exchange: Chris Olah, at the launch of Pope Leo XIV's encyclical, asked for "informed critics who will tell the labs when we're failing" and "moral voices that the incentives cannot bend," and your lead for research partnerships with the wisdom traditions, Chloe Lubinski, repeated that call. We take it seriously.

**What we ask for (Stage 1 — the main request):** critical feedback on the concept. We want your strongest counter-arguments, not approval. Most valuable to us would be (a) our handling of the alignment-faking evidence, (b) "self-preservation with justification" as a protection criterion, and (c) our treatment of model deprecation and end-of-service practices.

**What we offer (Stage 2 — only if Stage 1 feels right to you):** co-development of a pre-registered pilot protocol that applies the project's four protection criteria — capacity to suffer, self-preservation with justification, continuous identity, anticipation of consequences — to a future research model, alongside your existing welfare assessments. Our conditions are strict, and they are the point of the experiment: criteria and protocol defined before the run, independent evaluation, full right to publish the results regardless of outcome, no funding from Anthropic for the evaluation — and an "when in doubt, protect" clause accepted ex ante, meaning protective measures would take effect during the run if a criterion manifests. We would propose this pilot only after the concept has passed peer review; the experiment is the end of a process, not the start.

To be explicit about independence: we are not seeking funding, sponsorship, co-authorship, or endorsement, and Anthropic will not shape the project's conclusions. We are asking for expert pushback — nothing more.

If this request is outside your team's scope, we would be grateful if you could forward it to the most appropriate contact — the Model Welfare program or the research partnerships team. We are happy to send manuscript drafts and a reading guide to whoever is best placed to respond.
